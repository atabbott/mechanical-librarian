# Dynamic review lenses

The root assigns every initial reviewer one primary, source-specific attack
lens derived from the frozen changed surface. Record it in the canonical raw
packet and v3 review record. A lens directs the first and deepest investigation;
it is not a restriction on reporting. A reviewer may report any substantiated
material blocker encountered while following the evidence, even when it falls
outside the primary lens, but must not turn that permission into a second broad
checklist.

Choose orthogonal lenses within one review barrier:

- **Observable outcome:** start from user/operator output and trace backward.
- **Cross-layer value:** follow one decision-relevant value through every trust
  and serialization boundary.
- **State transition:** vary predecessor, retry, replay, cancellation, restart,
  and terminal provenance.
- **Grammar mutation:** probe valid near-misses, prefix/suffix confusions,
  namespaces, truncation, encoding, and ambiguous recovery.
- **Selection precedence:** vary ordering, duplicates, fallback, exclusion, and
  equally ranked candidates.
- **Identity and lineage:** mutate digests, source identity, cardinality,
  ownership, citations, and derived references independently.
- **Resource accounting:** make sequential/fallback work cumulative and probe
  exact and one-unit-over limits.
- **Forbidden effect:** begin from prohibited network, credential, mutation,
  disclosure, persistence, or authority and trace reachability.
- **History and contract:** compare final code/docs with accepted ADR history
  and authoritative external contracts.
- **Cumulative interaction:** ignore individual commits and inspect how all
  final changes interact.

Do not assign the same lens to every reviewer or force an irrelevant lens. When
a finding family expands, record why the earlier lens missed it and use a
materially different investigation technique for the expanded boundary.
